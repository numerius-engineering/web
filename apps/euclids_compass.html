Euclid's Compass v0.0.0 — Help & About

What this is

A single-file straightedge-and-compass construction tool — the same constructions Euclid works through in the Elements — with layers, an equivalence checker, and touch support. Everything runs locally in the browser; nothing you draw leaves this page.

Tools

  • Select — click a point, line, or circle/arc to select it. Drag a point to move it. Delete/Backspace removes it. Click a layer in the panel to move the selection there.
  • Eraser — click an object to delete it, or drag across several to erase a whole stroke's worth at once, without opening the Layers panel.
  • Point — click to place a point; snaps to existing points and computed intersections.
  • Line — click two points to draw a straight segment — the straightedge.
  • Compass — click a centre, then a radius point, to draw a circle. Toggle Arc to swing only part of the circle. Toggle Collapsing / Rigid to control whether the compass keeps its span after being lifted.
  • Transport — click two points to capture a length, click a centre, click a direction — a point is placed exactly that distance away.
  • Check — click two lines, two circles/arcs, or two angles to test them for equality, with the difference shown.

A selected line can be extended or trimmed to a new point, like sliding a straightedge further along its own direction. Layers can be locked to protect their contents from editing, deletion, or "Clear all." The canvas starts with two layers ready to go: Base (solid, red) as the active drawing layer, and Construction (dotted, black) waiting in the queue for auxiliary lines you don't want mixed into the main figure.

Euclid's tools, and how this app maps to them

Euclid's Elements opens with three postulates describing exactly two instruments: an unmarked straightedge, used only to draw a line between two points or extend one, and a compass, used to draw a circle with a given centre and radius.

Ancient tradition holds Euclid's compass was a collapsing compass — it lost its span the moment it left the page. You couldn't just pick it up and carry a radius somewhere else; that had to be proven possible, and that proof is Book I, Proposition 2. This app models both traditions: Collapsing mode requires re-measuring every radius from scratch, while Rigid mode holds its span like a modern drafting compass. The Transport tool directly implements the method of Proposition I.2 — reproducing a captured length exactly at a new location, in either compass mode. The Check tool mirrors the Elements' Common Notions about equal magnitudes, letting you confirm a construction's claims of equality instead of eyeballing them.

Using this app with the text

  1. Read a proposition's construction — Euclid gives it as a sequence of instructions ("let the line AB be drawn," "with centre A and distance AB describe the circle BCD," ...).
  2. Reproduce each step with the matching tool: Point for given points, Line for straightedge steps, Compass for "describe a circle," Transport for steps that copy a length.
  3. Use Check once you're done to confirm the proposition's equality claims.
  4. Use Layers to separate the problem's given elements from the construction lines you add to solve it.

A good starting point is Book I, Proposition 1 — an equilateral triangle built from two circles and a line — using only the Point, Compass, and Line tools in sequence.

The text, online

This app doesn't include Euclid's text itself. For the full Elements (all 13 books) with the original diagrams, see David E. Joyce's edition of Euclid's Elements (Clark University) — free online, with its own compass geometry tutorial that pairs well with this app.
